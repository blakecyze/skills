# Visual refinement

Treat these as inspection lenses, not a prescribed aesthetic. Existing identity, tokens, and coherent conventions take precedence.

## Layout and hierarchy

Check that related content groups together, labels sit near their controls, and spacing distinguishes groups from repeated items. Equal gaps are appropriate in homogeneous lists. Let actual content and action hierarchy determine density; do not force every screen into one primary action or a fixed container count.

Prefer shared alignment edges and content-driven breakpoints. Check narrow widths, long labels, empty content, and overflowing tables. Preserve access to clipped content. Remove containers or ornament when they compete with the task, not merely because they are decorative.

## Typography

Use the existing text roles and available fonts. Resolve weak hierarchy through spacing, weight, contrast, or size according to the composition. Inspect real wrapping and reading length; avoid shrinking text to rescue a layout. Truncated values need a usable way to reach the full content when it matters.

Use tabular numerals for changing counters when they reduce distracting movement. Check font loading, fallback width, and missing weights. Optical corrections earn their place through the rendered result, not a mandatory comment or exception rule.

## Colour and surface

Map repeated styles to existing tokens where that improves consistency. Distinguish selected, error, disabled, and primary roles without relying on colour alone. Preserve expressive brand colour when status and hierarchy remain clear. Check each supported theme rather than assuming inversion works.

Inspect actual foreground/background combinations, including opacity and underlays. Decorative boundaries do not all need the same contrast as controls. Use the applicable accessibility requirement for the element; a scanner's literal match is only a lead.

Keep corners, elevation, icons, and control proportions coherent within the design. Judge optical alignment in context. A border and shadow can serve different purposes; remove duplication when it adds no useful separation or expression.

## Copy

Use specific action labels, consistent terms, and honest status. Errors explain what is known and the available recovery without blaming the user. Empty search and first-use emptiness need different guidance. Preserve factual claims, commerce terms, and legal text unless editing them is in scope.
