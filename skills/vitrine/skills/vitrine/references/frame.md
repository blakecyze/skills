# Frame: angle, lens, crop

Labels: **Tested** rendered on one device and judged by eye. **Sourced** from a linked practitioner. **Observed** read from images. **Judgement** opinion. `W` is the object's width.

Compression comes from distance; a long lens just keeps the framing from further away. Product practitioners use 80 to 120 mm. Sourced: [Will Gibbons](https://www.willgibbons.com/realistic-rendering/).

| Family | Says | Camera that worked (Tested) | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three-quarter hero | Here's the whole thing | 85 mm, 18 to 26° to the side, 14 to 22° up, 4.5 W away | Cards, project headers |
| Straight on | Designed, precise | 135 mm, square to the main face, 6.3 W away | Spec sections, grids |
| Low / worm | Big, important | 28 mm, 1.4 W away, almost on the floor | Banners, title slides |
| High, rolled | Graphic, editorial | 85 mm, 62° up, rolled 16° | Social squares |
| Macro | Touch this | 105 mm, about 1 W from the detail, shallow focus | Detail rows, callouts |
| Off-frame crop | Confident; too good to fit | 55 mm, close, from the far corner | Cards, social |

## Rules

- Which side you see matters. Turning the camera to the other side opened the screen and threw the shadow across the frame. Tested.
- True top-down hid the screen on a wedge; a tilted high angle fixed it. Top-down suits flat objects. Tested.
- Low angles need the object big in frame. Far away, it just looks small: move closer and go wider. Tested.
- Show a shadow's whole shape or crop it decisively. A tip touching the frame edge looks like a mistake. Tested.
- Avoid aligning the product with background lines. Sourced: [Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/how-do-they-achieve-this-style/1615675).
- Crop phones instead of showing them whole. Pin windows to a corner and run them off the frame. Sourced: [Benji Taylor, Stageshow](https://benji.org/stageshow).

## Depth of field

Render scale matters. At 1.9 m wide, an 85 mm lens from 8.4 m at f/2.8 gives about 0.6 px of blur, so skip it at full-device framing. Calculated.

Macro values at that scale (Tested): f/0.09 turned everything to mush; f/0.55 was right 2 m from the keys; f/2.2 was right for the knob. Focus exactly on the hero element. Rescale these for another object.

## What earns a second look

Judgement, not data.

1. Something to discover. A macro rewards looking; a centred hero is understood and forgotten.
2. Implied touch: keys close enough to press, a knob mid-travel.
3. The product doing its job. A live screen beats a blank one.
4. A hint of a world, one cue only: a reflection, window light, or a table.
5. Tension: off-centre, cropped, rolled, or a big shadow.
6. Contrast between neighbours: wide next to macro, dark next to light.
7. A single idea that reads at 300 px. If it doesn't, simplify.
