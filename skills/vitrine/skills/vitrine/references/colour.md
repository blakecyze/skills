# Colour and grounds

Labels: **Tested** rendered on one device and judged by eye. **Sourced** from a practitioner. **Observed** read from images. **Judgement** opinion.

| Strategy | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Accent as ground | Signature shot, instantly recognisable | The device's orange: `#E23A00`. Tested |
| Object on its own colour | Calm, editorial | Cream on `#E7D9C2`. Tested |
| Ground from the screen | The screen is the star | Teal `#0E5A5F` from its shader. Tested |
| Dark field | Make a screen glow | `#101114`. Tested |
| Warm room | A place, a mood | `#3A2A20`. Tested |
| Complementary | Energy | Blue ground, warm-toned device. Tested |
| Surface from the product | Websites and apps | Yellow pebbles for a habits book, sand for a wearable. Observed at Metalab and Instrument |
| Colourways | Range, "this ships" | Cream, charcoal, orange. Label as concepts if not real. Tested |

## Rules

- Create a value jump first. A cream body at L\* 89 on a `#dde4ee` ground at L\* 90 had almost nothing separating them; every shot that worked had a clear jump. Tonal shots can get away with less only when a strong shadow draws the edge. Tested.
- One loud colour per frame. On an orange ground a colourful screen fought the image, and a neutral one made it. On a neutral ground, let the screen be loud. Tested.
- Make the accent appear twice. An orange key and knob on an orange ground tie object and ground together, while the cream body supplies contrast. Tested.
- Pick three to five grounds and keep to them, so variety still looks like one site. Judgement.
- Avoid averaged colours when tinting from images; they go muddy. Extract in Oklab with chroma weighting. Sourced.
- Keep the ground's top darker with a sweep or falloff. A pool of light adds depth with no extra objects. Tested.
- Bright light desaturates under AgX: a strong key turned an orange ground salmon. Use a deeper base and about 40% of the light. Tested.
- Check soft gradients for banding on phones. Sourced, weak; not checked.
- Check both site themes. A card on a pale page and on a navy page reads differently. Judgement.
